Canonical tags explained: how to check yours in two minutes

Many websites show the same content at more than one URL: with and without www, with tracking parameters, in a print view, or under several categories. A canonical tag tells search engines which URL is the main one.

<link rel="canonical" href="https://www.example.com/shoes/red-sneaker">

It is a strong hint, not a command. Google usually follows it, but may choose another URL if the signals disagree, for example when internal links and the sitemap point somewhere else.

When canonicals matter

  • Product pages that appear under several categories.
  • URLs with parameters such as ?utm_source=, sorting or filters.
  • The same article published on two of your sites.
  • http and https, or www and non-www, both still reachable.

Check any page in two minutes

  1. Open the page and view the source. Search for canonical.
  2. Check that the URL is absolute (starts with https://) and uses the right domain.
  3. Check that it points to a page that loads normally: no redirect, no 404, no noindex.
  4. On most normal pages, the canonical should point to the page itself.
  5. In Google Search Console, the URL Inspection tool shows both your canonical and the one Google chose.

Common mistakes

MistakeWhat happens
Canonical points to the staging or old domainGoogle may ignore the live page. Very common after a migration.
Every page points to the home pageUsually a template bug. Google may ignore the tag or treat pages as duplicates.
Canonical points to a redirected or noindex pageConflicting signals. Google picks its own canonical.
No canonical at allNot an error by itself, but Google has to guess on sites with duplicate URLs.
More than one canonical tag on a pageGoogle may ignore both. Often caused by a theme and a plugin both adding one.
Don't use a canonical tag to hide a page. If you don't want a page in search at all, use noindex instead.

Summary

  • A canonical tag names the main URL when content is reachable at several URLs.
  • Use absolute URLs on the live domain, pointing to working, indexable pages.
  • Re-check canonicals after every migration or theme change.
  • Use noindex, not a canonical, to keep a page out of search.
