How to find and fix broken links on your website

A broken link sends a visitor to an error page instead of the content they wanted. One or two won't hurt your rankings. But they frustrate visitors, waste crawler time and, when they pile up on important pages, make a site look neglected.

Where broken links come from

  • A page was deleted or its URL changed without a redirect.
  • A typo in a link added by hand.
  • A migration or redesign changed the URL structure.
  • An external site you linked to removed or moved the page.

What the status codes mean

CodeMeaning
404 Not FoundThe page doesn't exist at that URL.
410 GoneThe page was removed on purpose.
5xxA server error. Sometimes temporary, so check again before fixing.
301 / 302A redirect. Not broken, but long chains slow things down.

Which ones to fix first

  1. Internal links on your main pages: navigation, footer, home page and top landing pages.
  2. Old URLs that other sites link to. Redirect them so you keep the visitors and the link value.
  3. Internal links inside articles.
  4. External links to other websites. Replace or remove them.

The right fix for each case

  • The content moved: update the link to the new URL, and add a 301 redirect from the old URL.
  • The content is gone, with a close replacement: redirect to the replacement.
  • The content is gone, with no replacement: remove the link and let the old URL return 404 or 410.
  • A typo: fix the link.
Don't redirect every missing page to the home page. Google tends to treat that like a 404 anyway, and visitors don't find what they were looking for.

Summary

  • Fix internal links on your most important pages first.
  • Redirect old URLs that have links from other sites.
  • Remove links to content that is gone for good.
  • Check regularly, because new broken links appear after every change.
