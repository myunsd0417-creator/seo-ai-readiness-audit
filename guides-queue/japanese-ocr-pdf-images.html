Japanese OCR: turn scanned PDFs, receipts and images into text

Invoices from Japanese suppliers, scanned contracts, receipts and screenshots often arrive as images. Before you can search, translate or put them into a spreadsheet, you need the text. That is what OCR (optical character recognition) does.

Japanese is harder for OCR than English: thousands of characters, vertical writing, no spaces between words and small print on receipts. Here is how to get usable results.

First: does the PDF already contain text?

Try selecting text in the PDF. If you can copy it, the file already has a text layer and you don't need OCR. Reading that text directly is faster and has no recognition errors. Only scanned pages, which are just pictures, need OCR.

Tips for better results

  • Resolution: scan at 300 DPI. Lower resolution loses the small strokes that tell similar kanji apart.
  • Light and angle: photograph flat, straight on, in even light, with no shadows across the text.
  • Vertical text (縦書き): tell the OCR engine the text is vertical. Horizontal settings read vertical columns badly.
  • Mixed languages: choose Japanese plus English for documents that mix both, such as invoices with product codes.
  • Check numbers: amounts, dates and phone numbers matter most. Check them by hand.

Common OCR problems in Japanese

ProblemWhy it happens
Spaces between every characterMany engines add spaces as if Japanese were English. Remove them afterwards.
15.000円 instead of 15,000円Commas in small print are read as periods.
Similar characters swapped (ー and 一, ロ and 口)They look almost the same, especially at low resolution.
Columns read in the wrong orderVertical text was read with horizontal settings.
