Website migration SEO checklist: before, during and after launch

A redesign, a new CMS or a new domain is the most common reason a healthy site suddenly loses search traffic. The new site usually looks better. The problem is what changed underneath: URLs, redirects, robots settings and tags that nobody re-checked.

This checklist covers the steps that prevent most of the damage.

Before launch

  1. Export every current URL. Use your sitemap, your CMS export and your analytics top pages. This list is your safety net.
  2. Write a redirect map. Every old URL that changes gets a 301 redirect to the closest matching new page. Avoid sending everything to the home page.
  3. Keep what already ranks. Carry over titles, headings and main content for your best pages unless you have a reason to change them.
  4. Record a baseline. Save today's indexing, traffic and page scores so you can compare after launch.

On launch day

  1. Remove staging blocks. Test sites are usually hidden with noindex, a robots.txt Disallow: / or a password. Make sure none of these reach the live site.
  2. Check robots.txt on the new site, including whether AI search crawlers are still allowed.
  3. Test the redirects. Open a sample of old URLs. Each should go to the right new page in one hop, with a 301.
  4. Check canonical tags. They should point to the new URLs on the live domain, not to staging or the old domain.
  5. Publish the new sitemap and list it in robots.txt. If the domain changed, use the change of address tool in Google Search Console.
The most expensive mistake is also the easiest to miss: a staging noindex left on the live site. The pages look perfect in a browser and quietly disappear from search.

The weeks after launch

  • Watch Search Console for 404 errors and pages marked as excluded.
  • Fix redirect chains (A → B → C) by pointing A straight to C.
  • Update internal links so they point to the new URLs directly.
  • Compare traffic for your top pages with the baseline. A small dip is normal. A page that drops to zero needs a check.
  • Keep the redirects in place for at least a year.

Summary
