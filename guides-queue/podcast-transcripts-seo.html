Podcast transcripts for SEO: turn episodes into searchable pages

A podcast episode can hold an hour of useful answers, but a search engine sees almost none of it. If the episode page only has a title, a player and two lines of description, there is very little text for Google or an AI assistant to understand, quote or link to.

A transcript changes that. It turns spoken answers into text that people can search, skim and quote.

What a transcript gives you

  • Searchable text: every topic, name and question from the episode, in words.
  • Better show notes: you can pull headings, quotes and timestamps straight from it.
  • Accessibility: deaf and hard-of-hearing listeners can follow the episode, and people can read where they can't play audio.
  • Subtitles: SRT or VTT files for video versions and clips.
  • More content: blog posts, social posts and newsletters, all from one recording.

How to publish it well

  1. Give each episode its own page on your website, not only on the podcast platform.
  2. Write a short summary at the top: who is speaking and what the listener will learn.
  3. Add headings for the main topics, with timestamps.
  4. Put the full transcript below, fixing names and obvious errors first.
  5. Link to related episodes and to anything mentioned in the show.
Machine transcripts are good but not perfect. Read through names, numbers and technical terms before you publish. A few minutes of editing makes the page much more trustworthy.
